One atomic transaction

A keeper decides when. The borrower's on-chain policy decides what is permitted. Nothing here is custodial.

  1. Flash-borrow

    Take the loan token from Morpho for the length of one transaction.

  2. Repay

    Clear part of the borrower's debt, chosen to reach their target health.

  3. Withdraw

    Take the matching slice of collateral, capped by the borrower's own limit.

  4. Sell

    Sell it directly against a SparkDEX pool, inside the borrower's slippage bound.

  5. Settle

    Return the flash loan, pay the keeper fee, refund the surplus to the borrower.

If the pool is too thin to meet the bound, the transaction reverts and nothing happens. A protective action that refuses is the system working.

Liquidity is the binding constraint

This is the honest limit of the design, and it was measured rather than assumed.

Deleveraging only beats liquidation while the collateral sale stays cheaper than the penalty it avoids. Past roughly 300,000 FXRP in a single sale, it does not, and being liquidated is the better outcome. Ballast caps each sale and repairs a position in steps rather than pretending the depth exists.

Before you trust this with a position

  • The contracts are not audited.
  • The manager owner can propose a different swap adapter, but V3 accepts it only after the configured administrative timelock. Treat that owner as trusted until governance is in place.
  • Liquidity is the binding constraint. Deleveraging beats liquidation only up to roughly 300,000 FXRP in a single sale.
  • Protection depends on Morpho state, oracle updates, keeper uptime, and available Flare liquidity. The hosted keeper remains dry-run until a controlled mainnet receipt succeeds; a policy is not a guarantee against liquidation.
